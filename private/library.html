The challenge

The NLB Mobile App already had a large content catalogue: books, articles, videos, reading trails. But discovery relied almost entirely on search and category browsing, which works well if you know what you're looking for. For casual users and those open to exploring, it wasn't generating much engagement.

NLB wanted to explore genuinely new interaction models for content discovery, not just surface-level redesigns. The brief required a full end-to-end process: understand the user, identify the gaps, generate and test concepts, and converge on what worked. The constraint was to preserve existing IA patterns so new features could sit alongside what already existed.

01    Understanding Singaporean library goers

Before generating any ideas, the team needed a grounded understanding of how Singaporeans actually consume content — not what NLB assumed, but what users genuinely do. We ran interviews and research sessions to map behaviour into four distinct mindset categories: how people decide what to read, when they browse versus search, and what triggers or breaks the habit of engaging with content.

General content consumption mindsets

These mindsets became the evaluation lens for every concept we generated. An idea that worked for a goal-driven user but alienated a casual browser wasn't a good idea. The research kept ideation grounded in real behaviour rather than design preference.

02    Identifying problems and researching the market

With the user mindsets mapped, we identified where NLB's current experience fell short for each user type. Casual browsers had no good way to stumble on something new. Goal-driven users had to already know what they wanted before the app could help. There was no middle ground: a space for serendipitous discovery that still felt purposeful.

We then researched how other platforms solve this. Looking across library-inspired apps, content aggregators, and social media, three themes emerged: context-aware content surfaces better than static categories; personalisation needs to feel earned, not invasive; and habitual use requires hooks at natural re-entry points in a user's day.

03    Generating and shortlisting ideas

Ideas were brainstormed across three engagement stages: Discovery (sparking initial interest), Context (building relevance through user behaviour and goals), and Embed (making NLB a habitual part of daily life). Each concept was assessed against this framework to check whether it addressed a real gap or just added novelty.

Shortlisted ideas were then mapped against a prioritisation matrix weighing user appeal against technical and strategic feasibility. Two examples of concepts that made the cut:

04    Building interaction concepts and prototypes

With the shortlist confirmed by the client, each concept was developed into a testable prototype. A static screen can't tell you much — users need to actually interact with a concept to form a real opinion. For each model, I built a working prototype with enough fidelity and interaction logic that users could navigate it without guidance.

This meant pushing Figma beyond its standard use: variables to handle conditional states, smart animations for fluid transitions, and component logic to simulate dynamic content. Each prototype also had to clearly communicate interaction intent to the visual designers alongside me, so they could design the right states and edge cases.

05    Exploring visual design directions

In parallel with the interaction work, the visual design team explored four distinct directions for how the experience should feel, each with a different emotional register: editorial and structured, harmonious and calm, playful and bouncy, or empowering and confident. Working prototypes were built for the three shortlisted directions and tested with users alongside the interaction models, so we could understand which visual language resonated best with each concept.

06    Iterative testing and refinement

Testing ran throughout the project in multiple rounds, not just at the end. Early rounds helped us understand which broad directions resonated. Later rounds with higher-fidelity prototypes produced the evidence that determined which concepts to recommend to the client — and which to cut.

Running interviews was outside my core IxD remit, but I took it on because the team needed the capacity and because hearing user reactions directly made me a better judge of what to build next. Synthesis sessions after each round fed directly into decisions about what to develop further.

07    Converging on a final recommendation

After multiple rounds of testing, findings across all interaction models and visual directions were synthesised into a clear recommendation for the client. Concepts were plotted against audience fit and feasibility — the recommended concepts were those with the strongest user validation and the most realistic path to implementation within NLB's platform constraints.

The final deliverable included high-fidelity prototypes of the recommended concepts, incorporating the visual direction the team had converged on through testing, ready for NLB to use as a foundation for the next phase of development.
