The challenge

MINDEF needed a unified CRMS to manage cases raised by employees and NS men — replacing a fragmented existing system. The platform had to serve multiple user groups with different roles and permissions, while adhering to MINDEF's existing NS Hub Design Language System.

The team chose ServiceNow as the enterprise platform, but ServiceNow is designed for general use — its components didn't visually or behaviourally align with NS Hub out of the box. At the same time, senior stakeholders needed dashboards to monitor case trends and make decisions, and the chosen BI tool (Amazon QuickSight) had significant design constraints that required creative workarounds.

The core design challenge: adapt two different enterprise platforms to look and feel like one coherent product, while surfacing the right information to the right users.

ServiceNow is a powerful enterprise platform with many out-of-the-box components — but none of them were designed with NS Hub's visual language in mind. The team had to evaluate the NS Hub DLS and determine how to integrate it into ServiceNow without losing the platform's core functionality.

Dashboard workshop

To understand what the Senior Management dashboards needed to accomplish, we ran a full-day discovery workshop with MINDEF stakeholders. Rather than presenting assumptions upfront, we created two A0 scenario posters representing how managers would use the dashboard in practice — each built around a high-level goal. Clients used these posters as a drawing board, sticking post-its to surface and organise their pain points in a structured way.

Workshop scenario posters

Prioritisation matrix

After gathering pain points from the scenario activity, we prioritised them using a 2×2 matrix — weighing each item's impact on decision-making against how frequently the information would be used. This helped stakeholders visualise the relative importance of their needs and align on what to tackle first.

After the workshop, we synthesised the gathered pain points by clustering similar themes. This produced three key areas that guided the dashboard design direction:

Data availability & granularity

Insights around the lack of external system integration, data inaccuracy, and unavailability of key metrics.

Dashboard capabilities

Needs around functions and features to customise the dashboard view for different senior management roles.

Generative data & analytics

Opportunities around data visualisations and generative insights that could further support decision-making.

Dashboard design

The dashboards were built on Amazon QuickSight — a business intelligence tool with useful analytical features but significant visual constraints. Unlike a standard design canvas, QuickSight is largely static: every chart and component had to be manually positioned, and styling options were limited. The team had to be deliberate with layout, typography, and colour to make the dashboards feel cohesive and NS Hub-aligned despite these restrictions.

To keep the design process consistent across the team, we established three guiding principles that balanced the client's needs with the platform's limitations:

NS Hub colour

Maintain the NS Hub colour scheme throughout all dashboards to ensure visual alignment with MINDEF's design directive.

Visual impact

Go beyond presenting data — design for an aesthetically considered layout that motivates users to take meaningful actions based on the insights they gather.

Value amplification

Translate raw data into actionable information that directly supports the organisation's goals, rather than displaying metrics for their own sake.
